# 1. Abstract

We specify, calibrate, and document the deployment design of the first oracle-free perpetual futures contract on a path-dependent function of on-chain state: a perpetual on the rolling 30-day realized variance of BTC 1-minute log returns, settled entirely from the host venue's own trade history with no external price feed. The index is maintained in an O(1)-per-update ring buffer of approximately 350 kB, protected by a 4σ MAD outlier cap whose parameters are chosen to be inactive on normal data and binding on adversarial data. A Kyle-model manipulation-cost analysis calibrated to Hyperliquid BTC-perp order-book depth gives a lower bound of **\$3.7M per vol point** moved in the annualized index, with ≥93% cap attenuation of any single-minute shock at $k \ge 15\sigma$.

The paper's methodological anchor is an empirical negative result. A stochastic-volatility Monte Carlo calibrated to minute-level BTC returns reported 0% liquidation at 10× leverage across all margin tiers; historical replay against 1,124 rolling 7-day windows from January 2020 to March 2026 — a sample that includes the COVID crash, the May 2021 liquidation cascade, the LUNA collapse, FTX, SVB, and the spot-ETF approval — reported an **80% liquidation rate** at the same leverage. We trace the gap to the fundamental asymmetry of variance (bounded below by zero, unbounded above) and the well-known failure of diffusive SV models without jumps to reproduce tail behavior, and we adopt the Demeterfi-Derman-Kamal-Zou (1999) capped-variance-swap convention with a per-position 2.5× payoff cap as the canonical fix. Recalibrated against the capped historical distribution, both long-side and short-side empirical liquidation rates fall below a 5% gate at every tier, and the honest maximum leverage at tier 1 is **1.5×**. We argue this is the correct number — the right reference class is VIX futures, not spot perpetuals — and that the collapse from a nominal 10× to an honest 1.5× is the single most important quantitative result in the paper.

We specify the HIP-3 on-chain realization, including the oracle contract state layout, the per-minute update sequence, the cross-implementation determinism gate, a four-phase gated launch sequence, and the insurance-fund sizing. We generalize the construction to a four-condition framework (on-chain computability, manipulation-resistant estimator, meaningful capped payoff, natural counterparty flow) and sketch four worked applications — a gas-volatility perpetual, a BTC-ETH realized-correlation perpetual, a stablecoin depeg-risk perpetual, and a self-referential pre-IPO-style instrument — showing that the oracle-free path-dependent architecture extends beyond variance to a broader product family the existing on-chain derivatives stack cannot currently offer. All empirical numbers trace to deterministic scripts in the accompanying repository.
