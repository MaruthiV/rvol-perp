# 14. Conclusion

We set out to build the first oracle-free perpetual futures contract on a path-dependent function of on-chain state, using BTC 30-day realized variance as the prototype. Along the way we rediscovered — empirically, on six years of real BTC history — a result that Goldman Sachs's quantitative strategies group published in 1999 about equity variance swaps: the short side of an uncapped variance claim cannot be insured at any honest margin rate, and the canonical fix is a per-position 2.5× payoff cap. The rediscovery is the paper's methodological anchor. A stochastic-volatility Monte Carlo calibrated to minute-level BTC returns told us that 10× leverage was safe at tier 1; historical replay against 1,124 rolling 7-day windows told us that 80% of those positions would have been liquidated. The gap between the two numbers is the exact failure mode Bates (1996) warned about for equity indices, and it would have shipped into production if we had trusted the model.

The honest calibration that emerges from the capped-payoff recomputation is a maximum tier-1 leverage of **1.5×**, with both long and short empirical liquidation rates below the 5% gate at every tier on the 2020–2026 sample. This is the single most important quantitative result in the paper, and it is not the number we expected or hoped to find. We argue in §10.9 and §13.2 that 1.5× is the correct number, not a failure: variance is a high-volatility quantity, the right reference class is VIX futures (30%–75% margin, 1.3×–3.3× leverage) rather than BTC spot perpetuals, and the product's economic case rests on the +14-vol-point variance risk premium rather than on leverage. A trader who wants 10× exposure to vol should buy options; a trader who wants linear, oracle-free, expiry-free exposure to realized variance now has a contract specification that delivers exactly that.

The three contributions track the four difficulties identified in §2.2. (**C1**) The oracle-free path-dependent construction extends the Panoptic/InfinityPools principle from spot-dependent to path-dependent products via a 43,200-entry ring buffer with O(1) amortized updates and a ~350 kB state footprint. (**C2**) The Kyle-model manipulation analysis gives a lower bound of **\$3.7M per vol point** and a cap-reduction factor of ≥93% for any single-minute shock at $k \ge 15\sigma$. (**C3**) The empirical margin calibration is the paper's methodological centerpiece: a failed Monte Carlo, an 80% replay failure, an identification of variance asymmetry as the root cause, the adoption of the Demeterfi cap, and the Phase 8.6 recalibration that yields the 1.5× ceiling. (**C4**) The four-condition framework of §12 generalizes the construction to any on-chain-computable statistic with natural counterparty flow, and the four sketched applications — gas volatility, BTC-ETH correlation, stablecoin depeg risk, self-referential pre-IPO instruments — show that the variance perp is a prototype for an entire product family the existing on-chain derivatives stack cannot currently offer.

Two things we want the reader to take away beyond the specific contract.

First, **historical replay should be the calibration gate for any oracle-free derivative on an unbounded statistic**. Diffusive stochastic-vol Monte Carlo without jumps is systematically optimistic in the tail, and the tail is the only regime where the calibration matters. The Phase 8.5 failure is not a quirk of our particular model choice; it is the operational cost of trusting any model whose tail properties have not been independently validated against real regime-break history. Our recommendation is blunt: run the replay before the simulation, not after. If the replay passes and the simulation fails, investigate the simulation; if the simulation passes and the replay fails, stop and redesign.

Second, **the oracle-free property matters most exactly where the existing DeFi stack has been weakest**. Every large flash-loan attack in the DeFi oracle-manipulation literature [Qin et al. 2021; Eskandari et al. 2024] exploited a derivative whose settlement depended on an external or easily-manipulated feed. The path-dependent oracle-free construction removes that entire class of attack from a variance perp — not by making the oracle harder to attack, but by making the oracle be the chain itself. The same principle applies to any of the four product-family examples in §12, and we suspect it applies more broadly than the specific statistics we have worked out. A generic rule-of-thumb: if a new on-chain derivative requires an external oracle to settle, the design should be treated as provisional until an oracle-free alternative is ruled out.

The paper does not deliver a live contract; that is the deployment phase, and the launch sequence of §11.5 is the gate protocol for it. It does not deliver an ETH or SOL calibration; that is a parallel empirical exercise using the same machinery. It does not deliver a jump-diffusion SV model capable of reproducing the COVID-2020 tail prospectively; that is the future-work direction that would let the field calibrate against regimes that have not yet occurred. And it does not deliver a portfolio-margin analysis for dispersion traders running offsetting positions; that is a direct follow-up identified in §13. Each of these is within reach of the same procedures developed here, and we expect the most productive immediate extension to be the empirical validation of the product-family framework on a qualitatively different statistic — the gas-volatility perpetual of §12.2 is the most tractable candidate.

We began the paper by asking what the structural gap was between the variance-derivative needs of crypto market participants and the products the on-chain stack was able to offer. The gap was a linear, perpetual, oracle-free exposure to realized variance calibrated honestly against real history. The gap is now specified, implemented, calibrated, and ready for the deployment phase. The honest leverage ceiling is 1.5×, the short side is capped at 2.5× the entry strike, the oracle is the chain itself, and the calibration is the thinnest one the data will bear. Whether that product finds its counterparty flow in practice is an empirical question for the deployment phase, not a theoretical question for this paper. The architecture is done.
